Detroit-Style
Pizza Dough

Thick, airy, and fried crisp in a steel pan, with cheese caramelized into the edges and sauce striped on top. Pick your pan, dial in the crust, and choose a direct dough or an overnight poolish for a lighter, more flavorful crumb.

✓ The pan is the heart of the style

Detroit-style pizza is defined by its deep steel pan. The pan conducts heat to fry the bottom crisp, and its tall walls let the cheese melt down the sides and caramelize against the steel. That cheese edge is called frico, and it is the signature of the style. Your LloydPans are exactly the right tool for this.

✓ Cheese to the edges, sauce on top

Two things make a Detroit pizza look and taste like one. Cheese is pushed all the way to the pan walls so it caramelizes into the frico border. Sauce goes on top in stripes, after the bake. Put the sauce under the cheese and you lose the whole identity.

⚠ Plan your timing

A same-day direct dough takes about 4 to 5 hours start to finish. A cold ferment or an overnight poolish adds a day or more, but builds noticeably better flavor. Decide which path you want before you start, you will choose in Step 2 and Step 3.

What Is a Poolish, and Why Use One?
A small overnight preferment that builds flavor

A poolish is equal parts flour and water plus a tiny pinch of yeast, fermented 12 to 16 hours before you mix the main dough. It develops flavor and extensibility that a same-day dough cannot match, and gives a lighter, airier crumb. This calculator supports a straightforward Direct dough or a Poolish, and either one can be baked same-day or cold fermented.

Must-Have Ingredients
Bread Flour (12 to 13% protein)
Detroit dough is high hydration and needs the gluten strength of bread flour. King Arthur Bread Flour is ideal. All-purpose is supported, but the calculator drops hydration to suit it and the crumb will be a little less open.
Bread flour recommended
Cheese, to the edges
Wisconsin brick is traditional. Muenster is the closest easy substitute, and low-moisture whole-milk mozzarella is the easiest to find. You pick your cheese in the recipe section. Always avoid pre-shredded, the anti-caking coating ruins the melt.
Brick or substitutes
Thick Cooked Tomato Sauce
A cooked, slightly sweet tomato sauce, thick enough to sit in stripes rather than run. You can make it from San Marzano tomatoes or reduce a good jarred sauce. Options are in the recipe section.
Thick, cooked
Pepperoni
Cup-and-char pepperoni is excellent if you can find it. You can place half under the cheese and half on top.
Optional topping
Neutral Oil for the Pan
A thin layer in the pan before the dough goes in. This helps release and fries the bottom crust. The oil lives in the pan, not in the dough.

Tools
Essential
Detroit-Style Pan (8x10 or 10x14)
A deep steel or hard-anodized pan. Your LloydPans use a PSTK coating, so no seasoning is needed and they release well. The dark finish bakes hotter and faster than bare metal, so watch the edges and be ready to pull a minute early.
Kitchen Scale
Detroit dough uses small amounts of yeast, sometimes under 1g. Weigh everything. Volume measurements will not give consistent results.
Strongly Recommended
Metal Spatula or Thin Knife
For releasing the frico edge after baking. Run it around the pan walls to free the caramelized cheese, then lift the pizza out.
Wire Cooling Rack
Move the pizza onto a rack right after baking so the bottom stays crisp instead of steaming in the pan.
Recommended
Stand Mixer with Dough Hook
Helpful for the high-hydration, sticky dough. Hand mixing works too, it just takes more patience and a wetter feel.

✓ This is bake day, work through these in order

Your dough is already made. The steps below take you from the dough to a pan ready for the oven, then into the bake. The oven setup at the end depends on the baking surface you chose.

✓ Cooked sauce, spooned on top after the bake

Detroit sauce is a thick cooked tomato sauce striped over the pizza after it comes out of the oven. Make it while the dough proofs or the oven heats.

✓ Push the cheese all the way to the edges

The cheese touching the hot pan walls caramelizes into the frico border that defines Detroit style. Use cubed or shredded cheese (never pre-shredded, the coating ruins the melt) and push it right up to the corners.
