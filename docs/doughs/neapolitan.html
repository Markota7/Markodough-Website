Neapolitan
Pizza Dough

The original pizza. Four ingredients, high heat, 60 to 90 seconds. Choose between a traditional direct dough, a poolish for a lighter and more aromatic result, or a biga for a chewier and more complex crumb.

⚠ Plan ahead - this is not a same-day dough

Even the simplest Neapolitan (direct method) needs at least 8 hours at room temperature. Poolish and biga add an overnight pre-ferment before that. Cold ferment versions need 24 to 48 hours after mixing. Total time from start to bake: up to 3 days.

⚖ A kitchen scale accurate to 0.1g is essential

Neapolitan dough uses very small amounts of yeast, sometimes less than 0.5g. A fraction of a gram changes the fermentation speed and final flavor. Volume measurements will not work here.

✓ Four ingredients only

Authentic Neapolitan dough contains flour, water, salt, and yeast. Nothing else. No oil, no sugar, no diastatic malt. The simplicity is intentional. Flavor comes entirely from fermentation and high heat.

What Is a Preferment - and Why Does It Matter?
The key to flavor that same-day dough cannot replicate

A preferment is a small portion of your flour and water fermented before the main mix. Organic acids and flavor compounds develop slowly during this time. The result is a dough with more complex flavor, better extensibility, and a more open cornicione. This calculator supports three approaches, Direct, Poolish, and Biga. Each is explained fully before you commit in Step 2.

Must-Have Ingredients
Tipo 00 Pizza Flour
Look for Caputo "Pizzeria" or "Chef's Flour", approximately 12% protein. Do not use Tipo 00 pasta flour which is lower protein and produces a weaker, less extensible dough. All-purpose flour is supported but the result will differ noticeably.
Caputo recommended
Water
Fridge-cold if using a stand mixer. Filtered or low-chlorine tap water preferred, high chlorine can inhibit yeast activity.

Neapolitan dough uses 2.5% salt, higher than most pizza doughs. Do not use iodized table salt, iodine inhibits yeast activity.
Fine sea or kosher

For dusting your peel before launching. Neapolitan dough is softer and stickier than NY style. Semolina on the peel is essential, not optional.

Dough Containers
One per dough ball, lightly floured inside. Neapolitan dough balls are traditionally stored individually on a floured wooden tray or in separate containers.
Strongly Recommended
Pizza Oven
Neapolitan dough is engineered for 850 to 950°F. A pizza oven produces the authentic result, blistered cornicione, leopard spotting, soft wet center. A home oven is fully supported but produces a different result. The calculator adjusts hydration automatically based on your setup.
Recommended
Stand Mixer with Dough Hook
Required for biga, strongly recommended for poolish. Produces more consistent gluten development. Use fridge-cold water to offset friction heat.
Instant Read Thermometer
Target finished dough temperature (FDT) is 73 to 79°F. More important for Neapolitan than most other doughs given the precision required.
Pizza Peel (Wood or Metal)
Required if using a pizza oven or steel/stone. Use semolina on both wood and metal peels. Neapolitan dough sticks faster than NY style and the launch window is shorter.
Pizza Steel
Best home oven surface for Neapolitan. Place on the upper-middle rack as close to the broiler as possible. The broiler finish is essential when using a home oven.
Helpful
Infrared Thermometer Gun
Point at your oven floor or steel before launching. More important here than for NY style given the narrow 60 to 90 second bake window.
Turning Peel
A small rotating peel for turning the pizza at 900°F. Not essential but makes rotation noticeably easier in a pizza oven.
Bench Scraper
For dividing and handling dough. Neapolitan dough is softer than NY style. A bench scraper reduces tearing when dividing and moving balls.

⚠ Two things to do right now - before any cooking begins

1. Pull your dough from the fridge. It needs 2–3 hours at room temperature before shaping. Cold dough is stiff and will tear when you stretch it. Do not skip this. It cannot be rushed.

2. Set up your oven. Details below depend on your baking surface.

✓ Make your sauce while the oven preheats

Neapolitan sauce takes about 3 minutes. No cooking. Ever. The sauce goes on raw and cooks on the pizza in the oven.

✓ Tear, never grate - and drain thoroughly

Neapolitan pizza uses fresh mozzarella torn into thumb-sized pieces. Never grate it. Fresh mozzarella has high moisture content, draining before use is essential. Tear into pieces, place on paper towels, press gently, and rest for at least 15 minutes. Skipping this step leads to a wet center and a soft undercarriage.
